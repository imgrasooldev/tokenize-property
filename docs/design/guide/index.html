Section 1

Executive summary

What the platform is, who it is for, and where it stands today.

Each property is held by a trustee structure. Investors own units of that property as ERC-3643 security tokens on a permissioned blockchain operated by the platform. Rent is collected in rupees through a bank, split by holdings, taxed, and paid to investors' bank accounts. Every money or token action is proposed by one officer and approved by another, written to an append-only hash-chained ledger, and reconciled against the chain and the bank before anything is paid out.

Status, 24 October 2026. The core platform is built and running on a demo server with a live private chain. It is not yet open to real investors: KYC, bank, SMS and email providers are mock adapters, our own contracts have not had an external audit, and the legal wrapper (SECP sandbox, trustee agreement, SPV) is pending.

Which chain?

A private Hyperledger Besu network (Ethereum technology, QBFT consensus) with state hashes anchored to a public Ethereum Layer 2. Not Solana, not public Ethereum mainnet.

Who controls it?

Nobody alone. A 72-hour timelock owns every contract; multisig Safes for Admin, Trustee, Issuance and Operations sit behind it.

Where is the money?

In rupees, in trustee bank accounts. The chain records ownership and payout proofs; it never holds investor cash.

Biggest gaps?

External audit, real provider contracts, legal and SECP wrapper, and the final choice of public L2 for anchoring.

Public anchoring

Every 15 minutes the platform writes a StateAnchor record to a public chain: the latest private block number, its hash, and the hashes of the ledger and distribution state. Anyone can later take a private block and prove it matches the public anchor, without any investor data leaving the private network.

Today: the anchor target is a local stand-in chain (Anvil, chain ID 586650) so the pipeline runs end to end in the demo; more than 180 anchors are recorded. Production: a public Ethereum L2 (Base, Arbitrum or Polygon PoS), an owner decision; a few US dollars a month at 15-minute cadence.
Section 3

Smart contracts

Audited standard code does the token logic. Our small, non-upgradeable guards are the only things allowed to touch it.

ERC-3643 (T-REX 4.1) is the open standard for permissioned security tokens, used by regulated issuers worldwide. Unlike an ordinary ERC-20, every transfer is checked against an on-chain identity registry and compliance rules. We vendor the audited T-REX code unchanged.

Token, one per property

The units of ownership. Mint, burn, pause, freeze and forced transfer exist but can only be called by our guards.

ONCHAINID, one per investor

The investor's identity contract holding signed KYC and AML claims. Personal data stays off-chain; only claim hashes go on.

IdentityRegistry + Storage

Maps wallets to identities and countries. A wallet without a verified identity cannot receive tokens.

ModularCompliance

Runs rule modules on every transfer: CountryAllowModule (Pakistan, 586, only) and MaxBalanceModule (no identity above 10% of a property).

Design rule. All guards are owned by the timelock, emit an event for every state change, have no payable functions, no delegatecall, no selfdestruct, and are not upgradeable.

DistributionRegistry

Each rent distribution is published as a Merkle root of (investor, amount). A 48-hour challenge window follows; the Trustee Safe can reject. After finalisation the SHA-256 of the bank payout file is recorded once.

DocumentRegistry

Append-only hashes of title deeds, trustee agreements, valuations and court orders. Records are never modified or deleted.

StateAnchor (public chain)

Receives the periodic hash of private chain state. Anchored block numbers must strictly increase.

1 · Specification first

Every contract starts from the written spec: a table of functions, callers and behaviours.

2 · Invariants I-1 to I-17

Each safety property is a numbered rule: supply never exceeds the trustee cap; a frozen address cannot send; the owner of every contract is the timelock.

3 · Foundry tests

Each invariant becomes a unit, fuzz or invariant test. 137 tests pass; a failing invariant blocks the merge.

4 · Static analysis and findings

Slither runs in CI with zero open findings. Every finding gets a regression test.

Audit status. The ERC-3643 base is audited by its maintainers. Our guards and registries have internal review and automated analysis only. An external audit is required before real investor funds.

Through RecoveryGuard at any stage: the trustee proposes, a 48-hour delay runs, and the full balance moves to the re-verified wallet, even while the token is paused.

Section 5

Platform architecture

A PostgreSQL-centred TypeScript backend, one component that holds keys, a private chain for tokens, and three Next.js front ends. Hover or tap a block to see its job and what it talks to.

Database and security rules

Append-only migrations

31 checksum-protected migrations. A committed migration is never edited; fixes are new migrations.

State machines in triggers

Offering, exit, transfer and recovery transitions are enforced by the database, not only by application code.

Hash-chained audit

Every officer action is in a chained audit log with daily UTC digests; a verify function detects tampering.

Least-privilege roles

Each service has its own database role. The signer can read a bank file hash but cannot read bank tables.

Sealed personal data

Names, CNICs, phones and emails are AES-256-GCM sealed under keys outside the database, with separate keys for KYC, tax and notifications.

No single person, no single key

Hardware-key sign-in, step-up per action, a different approver, and a segregation-of-duties matrix with a verified digest.

Section 10

Investor and regulator FAQ

The questions investors ask, with the answers the system actually supports.

Is this a cryptocurrency?

No. Tokens represent units of ownership in a specific property held by a trustee. They are priced and paid in rupees, cannot be sent to anonymous wallets, and have no value outside the platform's legal structure.

Which blockchain is it on? Ethereum? Solana?

A private, permissioned Ethereum-compatible network (Hyperledger Besu) run by the platform, with its state hashes published to a public Ethereum Layer 2. Ethereum technology, not public Ethereum mainnet, and not Solana.

Why not a public chain where I can see everything?

Public chains expose every holding and wallet and require everyone to hold crypto for fees. Our chain keeps holdings private while the public anchor proves the records were not altered. You can verify your own entitlement on the verification page.

Do I need a crypto wallet or to buy crypto?

No. Your wallet is created and bound to your verified identity during KYC. You never pay gas or hold cryptocurrency.

Who can hold the tokens?

Only investors who passed KYC (CNIC, liveness, sanctions screening) and are resident in Pakistan. A transfer to anyone else fails on-chain. No identity may hold more than 10% of a property.

What stops the platform from minting tokens for itself?

Minting goes only through IssuanceGuard, which refuses any supply above the units on the trustee's register, and only a multisig with a trustee signer can call it. Supply is publicly checkable against the register.

Can the platform freeze or take my tokens?

A freeze requires a compliance case and the Ops multisig, recorded on-chain with a case reference. A forced transfer requires the independent trustee, a 48-hour delay, and an evidence hash (for example a court order) in the DocumentRegistry.

What if I lose my wallet or pass away?

The trustee runs a recovery: identity is re-verified or succession documents are filed, a 48-hour delay runs, and the full balance moves to the new wallet.

How is rent paid?

Rent lands in the trustee's bank account; expenses and withholding tax are deducted; the split is published on-chain as a Merkle root; the trustee has 48 hours to object; then a bank file pays your registered account. The file's hash is recorded on-chain.

What if the bank, the ledger and the chain disagree?

Reconciliation runs the three-way check before every payout. Any mismatch freezes all payouts until two operators investigate and clear it.

Who holds the keys?

No individual. A 72-hour timelock owns every contract; multisig Safes of directors, trustee officers and operations staff sit behind it. Automated keys can only pause, register KYC-approved investors, or write anchors.

Has the code been audited?

The ERC-3643 base is audited upstream. Our own contracts have internal review, 137 automated tests and static analysis. Two external audits and a penetration test are scheduled before any real offering.

Where is my personal data?

Off-chain, sealed with encryption keys held outside the database. The chain holds only claim hashes. Even support staff see masked data only.

What is the regulatory status?

Pre-pilot. The platform is designed for the SECP regulatory sandbox; the application, trustee agreement and SPV structure are pending.

Can I sell my tokens?

Today, through case-by-case transfers to another verified investor, approved by compliance and executed by the trustee with delivery-versus-payment. An open secondary market comes only after regulatory approval.

What are the main risks?

Property and tenant risk, liquidity (no open market yet), regulatory outcome, and the platform's own operational risk. The technical controls reduce fraud and error risk; they do not remove investment risk.
